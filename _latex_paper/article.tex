\section{Capacity Tests and Network Configurations}
\label{sec:appendix_capacity}

Ramp demand was first tested from 1{,}800 to 2{,}600~veh$\cdot$h in increments of 100~veh$\cdot$h. The range around the breakdown point was then tested from 1{,}800 to 2{,}050~veh$\cdot$h in increments of 50~veh$\cdot$h. A breakdown was defined as the queue exceeding 50\% of maximum storage for 20 consecutive control steps.

\section{Replication Assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}

The original study omits parameters and implementation details required to reproduce the experiments. The following assumptions were used:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Virtual edge lengths:} 200~m for the upstream mainline edge and 100~m for the upstream ramp edge.
    \item \textbf{Multi-ramp geometry:} 571~m between the Shiyang Road on-ramp and downstream off-ramp, based on satellite imagery, instead of the reported 397~m.
    \item \textbf{Maximum ramp storage:} 84, 112, 58, and 164 vehicles for Ramps~1--4, measured using red-light steady-state simulations with zero mainline flow.
    \item \textbf{Multi-ramp demand:} 8{,}000~veh$\cdot$h on the mainline and 1{,}400, 1{,}200, 900, and 1{,}600~veh$\cdot$h on Ramps~1--4, calibrated against the original speed heatmap.
    \item \textbf{Multi-ramp routing:} All vehicles use a through route and leave at the downstream end of the network.
    \item \textbf{Vehicle insertion:} Independent Bernoulli trials per lane and second approximate the macroscopic demand profiles.
    \item \textbf{HERO thresholds:} $q_\mathrm{on}=0.5$, $q_\mathrm{off}=0.3$, and $q_\mathrm{slave}=0.2$.
    \item \textbf{PPO architecture:} Hidden-layer width of 256 units, Sigmoid actor output, and initial log standard deviations of $-0.5$ for the single-ramp experiment and $-1.0$ for the multi-ramp experiment.
    \item \textbf{Multi-ramp state:} Only downstream occupancy, speed, and vehicle count are used; upstream mainline features are omitted.
    \item \textbf{State normalisation:} Fixed statistics computed from the no-control baseline are used.
    \item \textbf{Detector speed:} The mainline and ramp free-flow speed is 27.78~m/s. Empty-detector observations are imputed with this value.
    \item \textbf{Reward calibration:} $A_{\mathrm{TTS}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{TTS}}$ are computed from the replicated no-control baseline.
    \item \textbf{TTS calculation:} Vehicles waiting to enter the network are included.
    \item \textbf{Safety margin:} $\alpha=0.9$.
    \item \textbf{Penalty factor:} $c=0.1$.
    \item \textbf{PPO processing:} Advantages are standardised before each update and sampled actions are clamped to $[0,1]$.
    \item \textbf{Vectorisation and episode length:} One sequential environment is used without mini-batch shuffling. Single-ramp episodes use 240 control steps; multi-ramp episodes use 320.
    \item \textbf{Merge connection:} The acceleration-lane-to-mainline connection was removed because this produced the closest match to the reported ramp capacity.
    \item \textbf{Internal units:} Demand and capacity values are converted from veh$\cdot$h to veh$\cdot$s.
    \item \textbf{Training duration:} At least 500 episodes were inferred from the original figures because the paper does not report the number of episodes.
\end{itemize}

\section{State Normalisation}
\label{sec:normalisation}

The original paper states that input features are standardised but does not specify whether the statistics are updated during training. Static statistics computed from the no-control baseline and dynamically updated statistics were compared over 700 episodes and multiple random seeds. Their learning curves and final performance were statistically indistinguishable, so the static approach was used.
